**《The Weight of a Promise》**
*(Demo Test Script v0 · 8 Scenes)*

---

**S01 — The Setup Dinner**
A quiet corner booth at a mid-tier chain restaurant. **LIAM CHEN** (28, operations associate at a struggling startup) sits across from **GRACE SU** (27, a librarian with a gentle smile). Grace’s mother, **MRS. SU**, wastes no time with pleasantries.

**MRS. SU:** *(sipping her iced tea, eyes narrowed)* So, Liam. What do you do, exactly?

**LIAM:** *(nervous chuckle)* I’m in operations at a tech startup. We’re building a local services app—really exciting stuff, we just closed a seed round—

**MRS. SU:** *(cutting him off, setting down the glass)* A startup. So no 401(k), no health insurance through an employer, no guaranteed path to partnership or tenure. *(to Grace, pointedly)* Honey, you hear that? No safety net.

**GRACE:** *(quietly, embarrassed)* Mom, that’s not what matters—

**MRS. SU:** *(ignoring her, back to Liam)* In this economy, a man without a stable career plan is a man without a future. That’s just math.

---

**S02 — The Living Room Interrogation**
Later that week, at the Su family home. **MR. SU**, a retired high school principal, gestures for Liam to sit on the couch. The TV is off. The air is heavy.

**MR. SU:** *(leaning forward, hands clasped)* Liam, I’m going to be direct. We raised Grace to be independent, but we also raised her to be smart. Smart means marrying someone who can provide a *foundation*. A house with a fixed-rate mortgage. A retirement account that compounds. A career that doesn’t evaporate when the VC money runs dry.

**LIAM:** *(staring at the floor)* Sir, I understand. I’m working on it. I have a plan—

**MR. SU:** *(raising a hand)* Plans are cheap. Equity is real. *(pause)* You don’t have equity in anything yet, do you? Not in a home, not in a career track, not in a pension. *(Liam opens his mouth, closes it.)* That’s what I thought.

---

**S03 — The Price of a Promise**
A week later, at the same living room. Mrs. Su slides a piece of paper across the coffee table. On it, a single number: **$80,000**.

**MRS. SU:** This is what we expect as a *commitment deposit*. Call it a wedding fund, call it a test of seriousness—doesn’t matter what you call it. It shows us you’re willing to sacrifice for our daughter’s future.

**LIAM:** *(staring at the number, throat dry)* That’s… that’s more than I make in a year after rent and student loans.

**MR. SU:** Then you have a decision to make, son. We’re not asking for a handout. We’re asking for proof.

Liam doesn’t argue. He nods, folds the paper, and puts it in his pocket. Over the next three months, he takes on night shifts as a rideshare driver, weekends doing freelance data entry, and sells his vintage guitar collection. He loses fifteen pounds. He stops going to the gym. He stops answering calls from his old college buddies.

---

**S04 — The Missed Exit**
At the startup’s cramped office, **MARCUS**, Liam’s co-worker and friend, pulls him aside.

**MARCUS:** Dude, the founders just offered you the lead role on the new vertical—the one that’s going to be the company’s main revenue driver next year. This is *the* promotion. The one that gets you a real title, real equity, real everything.

**LIAM:** *(rubbing his eyes, exhausted)* I can’t take it.

**MARCUS:** What? Why not?

**LIAM:** Because it’s a long play. I need cash *now*. I need liquid, fast, no vesting schedule, no “trust me in three years.” *(pause)* I made a promise. I have to keep it.

**MARCUS:** *(confused, frustrated)* A promise to *who*? You’re killing yourself for what?

Liam doesn’t answer. Meanwhile, **GRACE** notices his distance. He’s always tired. He cancels dates last-minute. He forgets their anniversary. She starts to wonder if he’s lost interest—or worse, if he’s hiding something.

---

**S05 — The Breakup**
A rainy evening. Grace and Liam sit in his car outside her apartment. The windshield fogs up. Neither speaks for a long moment.

**GRACE:** *(voice trembling)* Liam, I need to ask you something, and I need you to be honest. Do you actually see a future with me?

**LIAM:** *(desperately)* Of course I do. That’s all I’ve been—

**GRACE:** Then why are you so *absent*? You’re never here. You’re always “working.” You don’t talk to me. You don’t plan anything. *(tears)* I feel like I’m dating a ghost.

**LIAM:** *(struggling, pride warring with honesty)* I just… I need more time. Please. Trust me.

**GRACE:** *(shaking her head, wiping her eyes)* Time isn’t the issue. You don’t have a plan. You don’t have a foundation. *(bitter laugh)* My mom was right. You can’t build a life on “trust me.”

She opens the door. Steps out. Looks back once.

**GRACE:** I’m sorry, Liam. I can’t wait for a maybe.

She closes the door. He watches her walk away, fists clenched on the steering wheel, saying nothing.

---

**S06 — Three Years Later**
*Title card: THREE YEARS LATER.*

**LIAM** is now the co-founder and CEO of **HARBOR**, a hyper-local services platform that connects users with vetted plumbers, cleaners, tutors, and dog walkers. The company just closed a **$200 million Series B** at a **$1.2 billion valuation**. He wears a simple black crewneck and jeans to the office. His name is on the door, but he doesn’t need to point it out.

**GRACE** is now a senior librarian at the city’s main branch. She’s good at her job. She’s respected. Her mother has set her up with three different “stable” men—a dentist, a CPA, and a civil engineer. All nice. All solvent. All… forgettable. She goes on dates, smiles, nods, and comes home to her cat.

One night, she gets a Facebook invite to the 10-year high school reunion. She RSVPs “yes” without thinking. She doesn’t know why.

---

**S07 — The Reunion**
A rented event space in a hotel ballroom. String lights, a mediocre DJ, and a cash bar. **CHAD ZHANG**—class valedictorian, now a mid-level product manager at a FAANG-adjacent tech giant—holds court by the appetizer table.

**CHAD:** *(loudly, to a small crowd)* Hey, remember when Liam Chen couldn’t even get an interview at a real company? *(laughs)* He was always “working on something.” Still “working on it,” buddy? *(winks)* I heard you’re still hustling. Good for you. Keep grinding, man.

A few people chuckle awkwardly. **GRACE** steps forward, jaw tight.

**GRACE:** *(coldly)* Chad, you haven’t changed a bit. Still measuring other people’s worth by your own insecurities.

**CHAD:** *(caught off guard)* Whoa, I was just—

**LIAM** walks up calmly, holding a sparkling water. He looks healthy. Rested. At peace.

**LIAM:** *(to Chad, with a small smile)* It’s fine, Grace. Chad’s not wrong—I *was* hustling. Still am, actually. *(to Chad)* Hey, I heard your company just lost its biggest client. That’s rough. Hope you land on your feet.

**CHAD:** *(blinking)* Wait, how do you know—?

Liam doesn’t answer. He just raises his glass to the group, nods at Grace, and walks away. Chad stares after him, suddenly very quiet.

---

**S08 — The Boardroom**
Two weeks later. **GRACE**’s library system is exploring a partnership with a tech company to digitize their catalog and build a community engagement app. The meeting is in a sleek downtown conference room. Grace’s boss leads the team in. The door at the far end opens.

**LIAM** walks in, followed by two VPs. He takes the seat at the head of the table.

**GRACE:** *(freezing)* …Liam?

**LIAM:** *(warm, professional)* Grace. Good to see you. *(to the room)* Welcome, everyone. I’m Liam Chen, founder of Harbor. We’re excited to explore how we can support your community programs.

The meeting proceeds. **CHAD**, now a junior account manager at a vendor firm, presents a competing proposal. Liam listens politely, then points out a single flaw in the pricing model—a hidden integration cost that would balloon the budget by 40%.

**CHAD:** *(sweating)* Well, that’s—that’s an estimate, we can—

**LIAM:** *(mildly)* It’s a contract line item, Chad. Not an estimate. *(turns to Grace’s boss)* We’ll match their functionality at 60% of the cost, with a guaranteed uptime SLA. I’ll have our legal team send over the draft by end of day.

The meeting ends. Grace lingers in the hallway. Liam steps out, hands in his pockets.

**GRACE:** *(quietly)* You never told me.

**LIAM:** *(shrugs)* You asked me for a plan. I didn’t have one yet. I had to build it first.

**GRACE:** *(voice cracking)* I’m so sorry. I should have—

**LIAM:** *(softly)* Don’t. You made the right call with what you knew. *(pause)* A 401(k) gives you security. But I wanted to give you something else. *(gestures to the building around them)* Something that can’t be taken away by a layoff or a market crash. Something I built with my own hands.

He pulls out his phone, opens a photo. It’s a framed certificate on his office wall: **HARBOR — FOUNDER & CEO — LIAM CHEN**. Below it, in small print: *“For Grace. For the future we didn’t have yet.”*

**GRACE:** *(tears streaming)* You named the company after me?

**LIAM:** *(smiling softly)* No. I named it after what you made me want to become. Someone worth waiting for.

She laughs through her tears. He offers her his hand.

**LIAM:** I’m not asking you to wait anymore. I’m asking you to walk with me. Same pace. Same direction. No more running.

She takes his hand.

**GRACE:** *(whispering)* I always wanted to. I just didn’t know how to believe.

**LIAM:** You don’t have to believe. Just look.

Behind them, through the glass wall of the conference room, the projector still displays the final slide: **HARBOR — FOUNDER: LIAM CHEN**. The name glows in soft blue light.

She looks at it. Then at him.

And for the first time in three years, she smiles like she means it.
